### The challenges

Core NetWars covers a wide range of security disciplines across four difficulty tiers, from introductory to hard. Challenges spanned forensics, log analysis and malware analysis, binary reverse engineering, cryptography and decryption, web application and network penetration testing, exploit development, Linux administration, Python and PHP scripting, and ICS process control and recovery.

SANS Amsterdam 2026 was my first NetWars tournament. In total I solved 53 challenges over the two evenings: 6 introductory, 9 easy, 34 medium and 4 hard. My strongest areas were forensics and log analysis, where I cleared every log analysis challenge, and binary reverse engineering, where I solved all seven medium challenges. On the hard tier I completed the decryption, DFIR and malware analysis challenges. In the end I finished in 2nd place with 1,680 points.


![Final Core NetWars leaderboard with XSS3cut10n3r in second place on 1,680 points](/images/netwars-amsterdam-2026/leaderboard.png)

### The most interesting: AI prompt injection

The AI prompt injection challenges were my highlight. Instead of exploiting buggy code, you had to talk a language model into breaking its own rules. It was really tough, especially within the timed constraints of the contest. With AI turning up in more and more applications, they felt like the most relevant challenges of the event.

### The people

The best part of the event was the people. I made some great friends over the course of the tournament, swapping ideas between challenges and comparing notes on the leaderboard. Meeting other people who enjoy these problems as much as I do made the whole experience.
